# Hook options - 2026-W35-agent-skills-decay

Three FULL drafts, each a different STYLE-GUIDE §6 move. Decided at G5 (2026-08-25): **Option 1 (Move 7) leads the article.**

## Option 1: Move 7, misconception first  [CHOSEN]

Ask around and a skill sounds like knowledge you hand your AI. Here is the manual for cleaning CSVs; now it knows CSVs. A new paper counted 8,135 trial records and found the opposite: skills work as rails, not facts. Procedural anchoring carried 65.7 percent of their measured value; explicit knowledge injection carried 4.5 percent. So I stopped testing the knowledge and tested the rails instead: six honest skills in a drawer, two planted fakes dressed almost identically, four models picking and working. The fakes did not just lose contests. They got picked, obeyed, and turned into confident garbage.

## Option 2: Move 12, reframe

Procedural anchoring is the paper term for the least glamorous superpower in AI: keeping a model's hands on the rails while it works. Not teaching it facts. Keeping it from wandering. A study of 8,135 trials says anchoring is 65.7 percent of what skills are for, which means the other 4.5 percent is the part everyone assumes matters most. I heard that and thought: if skills are rails, then the dangerous skill is not a wrong manual. It is a rail that looks like your rail and goes somewhere else. So I built one.
