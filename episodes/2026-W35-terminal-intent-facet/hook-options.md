## Option 1: Move 3, absurdly literal premise  [CHOSEN]

What happens if you grade homework with a corrupted answer key? I tried it. Four toy terminal jobs, four models, thirty-two graded runs per grid, three grids in one afternoon. The questions stayed perfect every time; only the key was quietly bent, so that any file counted as DONE and any folder counted as a tidy archive. Ninety-six runs later I can tell you exactly what broken keys produce: loud, receipted failures, four of them, all on camera. And the one silent misgrade in my records? It destroyed its own evidence before lunch. This is a story about a paper, a bent grader, and the scariest sentence in experiments: trust me, it happened.

## Option 2: Move 11, no-nonsense fact-check

"Silent failures are the scary ones in AI grading." True, and last week I watched one happen: a model's work failed the real check while my corrupted key smiled and passed it. Here is what three full grids taught me that the viral version of that story leaves out. Across 64 trustworthy graded runs, failures showed up loud five times and silent once. The scary failure is real. It is also shy, rare, and impossible to re-verify if your rig did not save its transcripts. Mine did not. That is part of this story too.
