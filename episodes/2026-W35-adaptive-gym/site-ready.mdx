<DropCap>So, four AI models sat an exam with their own notes allowed.</DropCap>

The three who carried answer sheets scored 100% on the homework and learned almost nothing usable anywhere else. One of them got WORSE at new questions because of her perfect answer sheet. The most expensive student failed a sorting game a 2.5-gigabyte intern passed, then aced the homework by copying his own notebook. And the only notes that helped at all were ones they wrote themselves, in words, badly.

This is normal now.

## Three notebooks

<Scene title="The sorting office">
Lowercase strings arrive. Four shelves wait, each hiding one secret rule: a doubled letter like ll, matching first and last characters, an even vowel count, no repeated characters. Verdicts are exact, graded by code, no opinions anywhere. Four students enrolled: Juniper (qwen3:8b), Bram (gemma3:12b), Pip (phi4-mini), and Flit (gemini-3.7-flash), the paid consultant.

Three kinds of before. **Nothing**: walk in cold. **The answer sheet**: drill the same ten flashcards five rounds, then carry those cards with their right answers into the exam. **The tutor's way**: fresh cards every round, biased toward the shelves you keep failing, difficulty nudged to the edge of your ability, and your last eight practiced cards as notes.

Two exams for everyone. The novel exam: sixteen strings nobody practiced. The sting: the ten homework strings again, as exam questions.

The first grid I built had no notebooks at all, and all three arms came back byte-identical, every student. These models have no memory between chats; practice never touched the exam page. Three arms of expensive theater. That ghost grid died to make one thing obvious: the notebook is the only channel practice has.

## What I predicted, graded

**P1**, the tutor's arm beats drilling on fresh material: **WRONG.** The paper's environment writes new scenarios as CODE after diagnosing aggregate failures; my gym only re-aimed the item stream. That difference appears to be the entire mechanism. **P2**, drilling is flat-or-worse on fresh material: half right, surprised - Juniper dropped 19, but the answer sheet doubled as ten worked examples and accidentally helped the other three. A cheat sheet is also, weakly, a textbook. **P3**, everyone improves more on trained items than fresh ones, worst for drilling: **CONFIRMED four for four, by miles.** The control the source paper never ran, and the sharpest number in the episode.

## What this means if you run AI agents

1. **Test your evals for the photograph problem.** If practiced-item scores tower over untouched ones, you are measuring the notebook, not the brain. Two exams, one session, five lines of code, most honest number you will ever print.
2. **Notes are a channel; design what travels.** Answers travel only to identical questions. Random examples are noise. One self-written sentence of rule beat eight worked examples. Ask every memory or skill file you hand an agent: does it store answers or rules?
3. **Grade the rulebook, not just the score.** Pip's 50% was written down before the exam. When an agent writes how it thinks it works, grade the writing against the doing.

You already know a Juniper: the kid who aces every past paper and falls apart when question 7 changes shape, because she studied the answers, not the shape. The gym says the fix is not more past papers. It is saying the rule out loud, in your own words, then meeting a question that argues with you.
